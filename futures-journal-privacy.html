Privacy Policy

Futures Journal — a trading journal for futures traders, by SideQuest Labs.dev.

Last updated: 11 September 2026

Futures Journal is a local-first Android app. Your trades, notes and screenshots are stored on your own device. SideQuest Labs.dev does not operate a server that receives your journal, and we cannot see your trading data. There is no account to create and no sign-in required to use the app.

This policy explains exactly what is stored, what leaves your device and when, and what our advertising provider receives.

The short version. Everything you log stays in private storage on your phone. It leaves your device in only two situations, both of which you control: if you connect Google Drive backup, and if you use Android's built-in device-to-device transfer when moving to a new phone. In both cases the data goes to your Google account or your new phone — never to us.

The app shows ads. The advertising SDK receives device and advertising identifiers, but it never receives your journal content.

1. Information stored on your device

The following is created by you and stored in the app's private storage, which other apps on your device cannot read:

  • Trades, including instrument, direction, entry and exit prices, stop prices, quantities, execution fills, fees, profit and loss, and R-multiples
  • Journal names, account names, instrument and session configuration, and trading fee settings
  • Notes, setups, emotions and plan-adherence labels you record against a trade
  • Chart photos and screenshots you attach to a trade
  • App settings such as your theme choice and which journal is active
  • Local safety snapshots taken before a restore or import, so a failed operation can be reasoned about

SideQuest Labs.dev does not receive or analyse your journal content, and there is no developer-hosted journal backend. The app transmits your journal only when you enable Google backup, export a file yourself, or use Android device transfer — each described below, and each under your control.

2. Optional Google Drive and Google Sheets backup

You can choose to connect a Google account so a journal is backed up to your own Google Drive. This is entirely optional, is offered per journal, and the app is fully functional without it.

When you connect Google:

  • Sign-in uses Android's Credential Manager. The app requests only the drive.file authorization scope. Under this scope Futures Journal can access files it creates, and files you explicitly select or open with the app — for example a spreadsheet whose link you paste in to import trades. It does not receive broad access to your Drive and cannot browse or list your other files.
  • The app creates a folder and a spreadsheet in your Drive and writes your trades, execution legs, configuration and any attached photos there.
  • These files belong to you. You can open, export or delete them from Drive at any time.
  • Your Google account address is stored on your device so the app knows which account a journal belongs to. It is not transmitted to us.

You can disconnect Google at any time in Settings → Backup & transfer. Disconnecting stops further synchronisation; it does not delete files already in your Drive, which remain yours to manage.

3. Photos and camera

The app can attach a chart photo to a trade, either by taking one with the camera or by choosing an existing image. The camera permission is used only when you actively choose to take a photo, and the app does not access your photo library in the background. Attached images are stored in the app's private storage, and are uploaded to your own Drive only if you have enabled backup for that journal.

4. Advertising

Futures Journal is free and displays banner advertising through Google AdMob. This is how the app is funded.

Your journal content is never sent to the advertising SDK. No trade, price, note, photo, journal name or profit-and-loss figure is shared with any advertising provider.

Google, as our advertising provider, may process data such as your device's advertising identifier, IP address, general device and network information, ad interactions, and diagnostics, for purposes including serving and measuring ads and preventing fraud. This processing is governed by Google's own policies. You can read how Google uses this information at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

The app declares the com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID permission, which allows the advertising SDK to access your device's resettable advertising ID. You can reset or delete that identifier at any time in your device's Android settings, under Privacy → Ads.

5. Consent and your advertising choices

Where required by law — including in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom — the app uses Google's User Messaging Platform to ask for your consent before personalised advertising is shown, and it records your choice.

You can review or change your advertising consent at any time in Settings → Ad privacy choices. Declining personalised ads does not restrict any feature of the app; journaling remains fully available.

6. Location

Futures Journal does not request any location permission and does not collect GPS or precise location data. Our advertising provider may infer an approximate, coarse location from your IP address, as described in Google's policies linked above.

7. Android backup and device transfer

These two Android features behave deliberately differently, and the distinction matters:

FeatureWhat it carries
Automatic Google cloud backup None of your journal. The trade database, your screenshots, local snapshots and app settings are all explicitly excluded from Android's automatic backup, so your trading data is never uploaded to Google's backup servers. Your journal reaches another device only through Drive backup that you switched on yourself.
Device-to-device transfer Your journal does travel. When you use Android's built-in transfer to move to a new phone, the trade database and settings move with it, so your new device is a complete copy. This is a migration between two devices you hold, initiated by you — not an upload to us or to a third party.

Your app-lock PIN is deliberately excluded from both. It is bound to a hardware-backed key on the original device and cannot be meaningfully restored elsewhere.

8. Sharing

We do not sell your data, and we do not share your journal with anyone. Data leaves your device only in the ways described above: to your own Google Drive if you enable backup, to your new device if you use Android transfer, and to Google's advertising services as described in section 4.

You may also export a journal to a CSV file yourself. Once you have exported a file or shared it, that copy is outside the app's control and is yours to manage.

9. Internet use and offline operation

The app works fully offline. An internet connection is used only to serve advertising, and — if you have connected Google — to synchronise with your own Drive and Sheets.

There is no SideQuest Labs.dev-operated backend. Network access is used for the Google advertising, consent, identity, Drive and Sheets services described in this policy, and for nothing else. Those Google components manage their own network endpoints, which Google may change over time.

10. Data retention and deletion

Because your journal lives on your device, you control its lifetime.

  • Deleting a single trade removes it from your journal. A small deletion record is retained — see the note below.
  • Permanently deleting a journal that is linked to Google does more than remove it locally. The app queues the deletion, moves that journal's folder in your Drive to the Drive trash, and then removes the journal's local records and attached photos once the cloud deletion is confirmed. Items in the Drive trash are removed by Google according to Drive's own retention rules, and you can empty the trash yourself at any time.
  • Uninstalling the app removes the app's private storage, including your local database and attached photos.
  • Uninstalling or disconnecting Google does not delete Drive files. Only the in-app permanent-deletion flow described above trashes them. Anything else you want removed, delete in Drive.

One honest technical detail: when a journal is backed up to Drive, deleting a trade leaves a small record that a particular item was deleted. This is what stops a deleted trade reappearing from another copy during synchronisation. It holds an identifier and a deletion time, not your trade content. It exists so that deletions are respected rather than silently undone.

11. Data security

  • Your journal is held in the app's private storage area, which the Android operating system isolates from other apps.
  • You can enable an app lock using biometrics or a PIN. The PIN verifier is protected using Android Keystore and never leaves your device.
  • All network communication with Google's services uses encrypted HTTPS connections.
  • Access to your Drive is limited to the narrow drive.file scope, so the app reaches only the files it created and the files you explicitly open with it.

No method of electronic storage or transmission is perfectly secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.

12. Analytics and tracking

Futures Journal contains no analytics SDK, no crash-reporting SDK and no attribution or marketing SDK. We do not track how you use the app, we receive no usage statistics, and we receive no crash reports. Aside from the advertising described in section 4, there are no third-party trackers in this app.

13. Third-party services

The app uses the following Google services, each governed by Google's own privacy policy at policies.google.com/privacy:

  • Google AdMob and the User Messaging Platform — advertising and consent
  • Google Play services and Credential Manager — optional sign-in
  • Google Drive and Google Sheets — optional backup to your own account

14. Children's privacy

Futures Journal is a tool for futures traders and is not directed at children. It is not intended for use by anyone under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from children. If you believe a child has provided information through this app, please contact us.

15. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, export or delete personal data held about you. Because your journal is stored on your own device and in your own Google Drive, you can exercise these directly: view and edit your data in the app, export it to CSV, and delete it from the app and from Drive. For anything relating to advertising data held by Google, use the advertising controls described in section 5 and Google's own privacy tools.
